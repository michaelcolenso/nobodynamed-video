# Next-batch editorial dossier — 2025 SSA release

This document is the second-stage review of the first data-first hopper run. The hopper is a discovery heuristic; this review asks whether each curve can support a concise, defensible story without turning timing into causation.

## Advance to story drafting

### Rihanna — cultural rupture

**Data signal:** first reported in 2000; largest one-year shock lands in 2005; peak 1,075 in 2008; 87 in 2025.

**External timing:** Apple Music dates “Pon de Replay” to May 24, 2005. Official Charts records its first UK chart date in September 2005 and a peak at #2.

**Thesis:** Rihanna was already present in the SSA record, but 2005 is the visible break in the curve. The singer’s breakout and the name’s sharp jump align in time; that alignment is evidence of a cultural moment, not proof that every parent chose the name because of her.

**Draft angle:** “You can see Rihanna arrive.”

**Sources:**
- https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html
- https://music.apple.com/us/song/1440867324
- https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/rihanna-pon-de-replay/

### Miley — comeback with a cultural first act

**Data signal:** largest one-year shock lands in 2006; peak 2,652 in 2008; 900 in 2025; 2025 is up 144% from 2020.

**External timing:** Disney records the Hannah Montana premiere on March 24, 2006 and describes the show as the launch of Miley Cyrus into teen-idol fame. “Flowers” later debuted at #1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 2023.

**Thesis:** Miley’s first surge aligns with Hannah Montana, but the more surprising story is the second act: the name fell far below its 2008 peak and then began climbing again. Recent visibility is context, not a claimed cause of the rebound.

**Draft angle:** “Miley came back.”

**Sources:**
- https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html
- https://d23.com/a-to-z/hannah-montana-television/
- https://ca.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/miley-cyrus-flowers-ties-most-weeks-number-one-adult-contemporary-chart-1235576436/

### Aaliyah — cultural rupture that kept growing

**Data signal:** largest one-year shock is 64.955x in 1994; peak 5,523 in 2012; 2,607 in 2025.

**External timing:** Aaliyah’s debut album, Age Ain’t Nothing but a Number, was released in 1994. GRAMMY’s retrospective describes 1994 as the start of her recording career and 1996 as the beginning of a new artistic era.

**Thesis:** The singer’s 1994 debut aligns with an extraordinary one-year break in the name curve, but the name did not merely spike and disappear. It continued growing and did not peak until 2012.

**Draft angle:** “Aaliyah outgrew the moment that launched it.”

**Sources:**
- https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html
- https://music.apple.com/us/album/age-aint-nothing-but-a-number-deluxe/254938506
- https://www.grammy.com/news/aaliyah-one-million-record-25th-anniversary-record/

### Elsa — narrow post-Frozen peak

**Data signal:** peak 1,140 in 2014; 216 in 2025; half-peak width is only two years.

**External timing:** Disney dates Frozen to November 27, 2013 and identifies Elsa as a central character. The Academy lists Frozen as the 2014 Best Animated Feature winner.

**Thesis:** Elsa’s national birth count peaks immediately after Frozen became a major cultural event. The timing is unusually tight, but the story should say the peak followed the film—not that the film alone caused it.

**Draft angle:** “Elsa peaks one year after Frozen.”

**Sources:**
- https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html
- https://movies.disney.com/frozen/
- https://www.oscars.org/events/animated-features

### Leia — the event is visible, but it is not the peak

**Data signal:** a 3.667x one-year shock lands in 1977; peak 1,424 comes much later in 2023; 1,033 in 2025.

**External timing:** Star Wars opened May 25, 1977. StarWars.com identifies Princess Leia Organa as a central hero of A New Hope and the broader franchise.

**Thesis:** Star Wars leaves a visible 1977 pulse in the Leia curve, but Leia’s actual national peak arrives 46 years later. That makes the story more interesting than a simple movie-created-name claim.

**Draft angle:** “Star Wars moved Leia. It did not peak it.”

**Sources:**
- https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html
- https://www.starwars.com/films/star-wars-episode-iv-a-new-hope/
- https://www.starwars.com/databank/leia-organa

### Atlas — ancient name, modern record

**Data signal:** first reported in 1883; intermittent historical gaps; 4,413 boys in 2025, its all-time peak; up 115% from 2020 and more than 6x from 2015.

**External context:** Atlas is an ancient Greek mythological name, not a newly coined modern name.

**Thesis:** Atlas is a resurrection story disguised as a modern invention. The SSA record reaches back to the nineteenth century, yet 2025 is its highest year ever.

**Draft angle:** “Atlas is old. Its peak is now.”

**Sources:**
- https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html
- https://www.behindthename.com/name/atlas

## Hold for a later batch

### Debra — spectacular generational collapse

**Data signal:** peak 50,563 in 1955; 31 in 2025; peak-to-latest ratio 1,631x.

**Thesis:** Debra is one of the cleanest examples of a name belonging to a specific American generation.

**Why hold:** excellent curve, but the launch batch already contains generational-collapse stories. Use when the feed needs a pure data-history reset.

**Context source:** https://www.behindthename.com/name/debra

### Gary — a male generational collapse with historical context

**Data signal:** peak 38,754 in 1952; 201 in 2025; peak-to-latest ratio 193x.

**External context:** Behind the Name cites dictionary sources describing Gary Cooper as a major popularizer of the name in the late 1920s and notes its strong 1940s–1950s popularity.

**Thesis:** Gary became a defining mid-century male name, then steadily aged out of the nursery.

**Why hold:** strong but overlaps Debra/Jennifer structurally; useful as a later male counterpart.

**Context source:** https://www.behindthename.com/name/gary

### Ashley — the paired-sex story needs first-class support

**Data signal:** 2025 reported counts are F 1,829 / M 27. Male peak: 746 in 1980. Female peak: 54,856 in 1987.

**External context:** Ashley is historically both masculine and feminine; Behind the Name notes that it was more commonly given to boys in the United States until the 1960s and later became predominantly feminine.

**Thesis:** Ashley is not simply a girls’ name that was once occasionally used for boys; the two SSA curves show a genuine gender migration.

**Why hold:** this deserves a paired-sex story primitive rather than forcing two series into a single-sex StorySpec. Add that capability, then promote it.

**Context source:** https://www.behindthename.com/name/ashley

### Khaleesi — persistence is more interesting than the expected collapse

**Data signal:** first reported in 2011; peak 565 in 2018; 410 in 2025.

**External timing:** Khaleesi is the Dothraki title associated with Daenerys Targaryen in Game of Thrones; the television adaptation ran 2011–2019.

**Thesis:** The counterintuitive story is survival: Khaleesi did not disappear when Game of Thrones ended and remains close to its peak years.

**Why hold:** strong reserve, but the next six already contain several culture-linked stories. It becomes more valuable after the first culture-heavy batch establishes a baseline.

**Sources:**
- https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/limits.html
- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ydi80YrCnPY
- https://www.behindthename.com/name/khaleesi/

## Promotion decision

Advance **Rihanna, Miley, Aaliyah, Elsa, Leia, and Atlas** to draft StorySpecs. Keep **Debra, Gary, Ashley, and Khaleesi** in the evidence-backed reserve.

Before any of the six can reach release approval, bind all annual count claims to fresh official SSA snapshots from the same 2025 names.zip archive used for the launch batch. Cultural-rupture stories must retain at least two non-SSA sources and must describe temporal alignment rather than causation unless stronger evidence exists.
